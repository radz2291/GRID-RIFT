# 01 · Vision

Status: foundation agreed; game details are provisional. Version: 0.1.0.

## The experience

GRID-RIFT places a participant in an open-air technological wilderness. They control a unit, read incomplete information, choose a course of action, respond to opponents, and live with the consequences. Other participants may be people, local scripted policies, or later learned policies.

The battlefield should create consequential choices, not merely a sequence of targets to click. A player might secure a resource, rescue an ally, delay an opponent, or protect an escape route. They cannot do everything at once. Good feedback makes it possible to understand what happened without revealing information the participant was not entitled to know.

## What we are building first

A small single-player browser encounter against local AI. Then a compact 3v3 scenario: one human with two AI allies against three AI enemies, two lanes, a central contest, and a clear end condition. Approximate eventual match target: 10–15 minutes. These are experiment settings, not engine laws.

Before the larger battlefield, deliver a 1v1 encounter with one playable archetype, movement, basic attack, two abilities, two tactical routes, a contested objective, restart, and readable feedback. Do not begin with six heroes and eighteen items. Expand only when a missing decision justifies a mechanic.

## What makes it distinct

The space is outdoors: visible sky, broad horizons, rock-like formations, open plateaus, and technological light integrated into the landscape. It must not feel like a server room, indoor corridor, or enclosed cyberpunk street. Original assets and names are required; references describe qualities rather than content to copy.

Depth comes from interacting constraints: information, time, position, resources, commitments, and other participants. Opponents should be challenging through observable choices, not concealed advantages. Presentation supports the decisions and can be replaced without changing their outcome.

## Success

An owner can launch a small playable encounter and identify a meaningful decision they would make differently next time. A developer can change the victory rule, participant count, policy, or presentation without rewriting unrelated systems. A run can be inspected and replayed within its declared compatibility scope. Evaluation explains narrow task performance rather than issuing universal labels about a person or agent.

## Non-goals for the first delivery

A commercial-scale Dota replacement, an enormous hero roster, online competitive infrastructure, photorealism, real-world combat modelling, paid AI integration, and claims of comprehensive psychological assessment. Behavioural state in an agent is a simulation mechanism, not a claim that the agent experiences feelings.

## Decision priority

Preserve meaningful choices and honest feedback. Preserve fair information access and reproducibility. Keep architecture replaceable. Maintain controllability and readability. Add aesthetic richness and content breadth when those foundations hold.

An uncomfortable control scheme is not a desirable difficulty setting. Visual excitement is welcome, but neither spectacle nor frustration is evidence that the intended decision challenge exists.

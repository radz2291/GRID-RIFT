# 07 · World and visual direction

## Identity

An open technological wilderness beneath a visible sky. Clean luminous accents run through terrain and structures, but the world feels outdoors rather than inside a computer room. Use original shapes, names, symbols, and assets. A film or game reference is an influence, not permission to copy its distinctive content.

Imagine an open twilight plateau: pale rock-like ridges, dark graphite ground, sparse luminous vegetation, broad sky, and distant monumental structures. The arena has room to breathe. Technology is integrated into a landscape rather than replacing it with endless polished floors.

## Composition and camera

Start with a tilted top-down camera that exposes the battlefield clearly. Keep a stable orientation, bounded pan, controllable zoom, a centre-on-unit command, and sufficient view of immediate escape paths. Camera angle and distance are provisional, to be judged during play.

At normal play zoom, silhouettes, objective zones, and blockers must read before decorative detail. Distant structures and horizon cues establish outdoor scale without obscuring the active arena. Tall foreground objects must not hide units; fade or cut them in presentation without changing line-of-sight rules.

## Material and light

Use restrained emissive seams, not neon on every surface. Cyan can support environment technology, with distinct friendly/enemy markers that remain readable without colour alone. Keep combat telegraphs brighter and clearer than decorative lights. Avoid excessive bloom, mirror-like floors, harsh black voids, deep indoor shadows, or cluttered particles.

Terrain should look clean and dimensional, not dirty or visually noisy. Rock forms can have technological edges or inlays. Sparse vegetation can suggest energy-driven ecology. Light and atmosphere may imply scale without resembling a ceiling.

## Never create the wrong environment

No ceiling, enclosed corridor maze, server racks, industrial interior, cyberpunk alley, or shiny floor that makes the world read as an indoor hall. Avoid solving the outdoor direction by placing an indoor arena inside a skybox.

## Readability is functional

Show entity facing, health where applicable, attacks, hit confirmation, selected target, cooldowns, objective ownership, and intended move destination. Use shape, motion, border style, or icon in addition to colour. Normal UI and sound receive only permitted information; a hidden opponent cannot become detectable through particle trails or a floating health bar.

A hit effect acknowledges a simulation event. A ground warning corresponds to a simulation-defined danger area. Do not enlarge a telegraph for visual drama without explicitly preserving its meaning.

## Production order

First: simple geometry, accurate collision/vision data, clear units, functional input, basic sky and ground. Second: readable attacks, objective state, HUD, selection, and feedback. Third: consistent art language, atmosphere, restrained effects, and sound. Cosmetic polish cannot replace a broken tactical loop.

Proposed interactive target: stable, responsive play on an ordinary desktop browser. Measure on named hardware, resolution, browser, population, and settings before setting a firm frame-rate acceptance threshold. Lowering visual quality must not change world outcomes. No performance claim is established by this handoff.

## Visual acceptance questions

Does one screenshot read as outdoors? Can a player see where they can move? Can they distinguish an attack warning from decoration? Can they identify the objective and escape route? Does the same authoritative encounter remain playable with effects reduced? These require actual screenshots and play evidence in Stage 1, not an architectural claim.
